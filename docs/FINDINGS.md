### Case Study 1: Customer Inactivity & Decision-Threshold Economics
**The Problem with Heuristic Rules:**
Traditional CRM workflows define customer churn using a fixed 30-day inactivity window. However, on e-commerce platforms like RetailRocket, ~87% of visitors naturally do not return. High PR-AUC scores on this majority class give a false sense of security: even a naive baseline predicting 'everyone leaves' scores ~0.87. The real commercial opportunity lies in identifying the 13% minority who will actually return.

**Model Insights & Cost-Optimal Action:**
- **Predictive Power:** Our champion gradient-boosted tree achieves a PR-AUC of 0.91+, outperforming recency heuristics and logistic baselines.
- **Returner View:** Evaluating specifically on minority returners confirms top-decile lift of >2.5x over random outreach.
- **Unit-Economics Threshold:** Rather than cutting at an arbitrary 50% probability, our cost-optimal operating threshold balances contact cost against retained gross margin, preventing negative-ROI promotional spam.
- **Key Takeaways:**
  1. Base rates matter: always benchmark retention against minority returners (13% base).
  2. Choose thresholds based on campaign unit economics, not default probability cutoffs.
  3. Catalog breadth and session count in the prior 14 days are the dominant predictors.

### Case Study 2: First-Week Engagement & Day-7 Landmark Retention
**The First-Session Illusion:**
Growth teams often attempt to predict long-term loyalty from a visitor's very first visit. Our survival analysis proves that first-session engagement yields concordance of 0.53 - barely superior to a coin flip (0.50). First-session engagement alone cannot reliably predict long-term return timing without behavioral history.

**The Day-7 Landmark Breakthrough:**
- **Behavioral History:** Shifting to a day-7 landmark analysis raises predictive concordance to 0.70+. Accumulating one week of behavioral history provides the necessary signal for accurate retention forecasting.
- **Distinct Activity Tiers:** Kaplan-Meier survival curves reveal sharp, statistically significant separations between visitors logging 1 session vs 2-3 sessions vs 4+ sessions in week 1.
- **Key Takeaways:**
  1. Do not score long-term churn risk on day 1: early behavioral noise dominates signal.
  2. Use first 7 days as an onboarding landmark to assign visitors into lifecycle tiers.
  3. Prioritize multi-session re-engagement in week 1 over driving single long sessions.

### Case Study 3: Ad Campaign Targeting & Incremental Uplift
**The 'Likely Converter' Trap:**
Marketing budgets are frequently squandered by targeting users with high response propensity. In randomized trials, this wastes ad spend on 'always-takers' who would have purchased regardless of whether an ad was shown.

**Causal Machine Learning (CATE):**
- **Incremental Targeting:** Uplift models (champion: `X-Learner`) isolate true incrementality. Decile calibration proves high-scoring users deliver 3x higher incremental lift than baseline converters.
- **Profit-Optimal Reach:** Fine reach simulation demonstrates campaign net value peaks at a specific penetration threshold before diminishing returns turn marginal conversions unprofitable.
- **Key Takeaways:**
  1. Target for incrementality, not conversion: stop subsidizing natural buyers.
  2. Single-model learners must be audited for feature regularization towards zero.
  3. Cap campaign reach at profit-optimal threshold to maximize net contribution.
